# RQ1–RQ3: organization-issued asset authority (draft a1)

**Status:** synthetic research slice; all 25 AA cases in
`tests/cases/rq123_cases.toml` are **reviewed** for their stated synthetic
outcomes (AA01–AA24 after the first 24/24 run, AA25 after its runner landed and
the 25/25 run). This slice has a separate `AssetAuthority` decision. It does not
change `ValidChain_P`, session redemption, the RQ6 mediator, or any previously
reviewed case.

## RQ1 — trust anchor assumption

Provider P manually verifies organization O and an authorized representative
outside this prototype. P issues an Ed25519 root key to O and pins its public key
in P's `RootRegistry`. This is an **assumption**, not an experiment showing that
manual vetting occurred. If P generated the private key, the model additionally
trusts P's delivery and deletion of its signing copy; the simulation does not
prove either. A root slot supplied with a request cannot establish the anchor.

For this slice, P assumes that O, through its root and authorized delegates, may
declare which synthetic assets belong in O's testing authorization namespace.
P does **not** independently establish real-world ownership/control of each
asset. A signed declaration establishes who made the claim and its permitted
scope under this assumption, not legal ownership or live possession of a domain.

## RQ2 — signed authority and content identity

Separate raw objects are signed with Ed25519 over
`"aisig-v0" || 0x00 || TypeTag || 0x00 || canonical_payload`. The three new type
tags are `RoleDelegation`, `AssetRecord`, and `AssetRequest`. The public decision
API takes raw canonical envelope bytes only. The same strict JSON rules as the
existing chain apply (duplicate keys, nulls, floats, noncanonical encodings and
wrong type tags fail closed).

* A `RoleDelegation` binds the issuer's key to a subject public key and
  `subject_user_id`, role, exact parent reference `(id, version, digest)`,
  audience, organization, allowed assets/classes, `may_delegate`,
  `may_register_asset`, and validity window. A root signs CTO; CTO signs Senior;
  Senior signs Junior. The displayed role is checked against that sequence but
  grants no implicit privilege. Each child scope/class/window must be contained
  in its parent's; the signer must have `may_delegate`.
* A CTO or Senior with `may_register_asset` signs an `AssetRecord`, referring
  to **one exact delegation on the same presented path**, the exact Junior
  delegation, an asset, version, permitted classes, validity, and a manifest
  digest. Its asset/classes/window must fit both the registrar's and Junior's
  delegations. A record signed under another path is not transferable.
* Junior signs an `AssetRequest` with `subject_user_id`, asset, requested class,
  exact record and leaf references, and manifest digest. P verifies the
  signature under the Junior key conveyed by this path. This demonstrates
  request-key possession in the synthetic model; it is not an RQ5 session.

The digest is

`SHA-256("aisig-asset-manifest/v1" || 0x00 || exact canonical AssetManifest bytes)`.

`AssetManifest` contains a typed asset, `asset_version`, and synthetic
`snapshot_marker`. P recomputes the digest from the supplied bytes and compares
it to the signed record and request. This pins **one declared snapshot**, like a
content address. The marker is public synthetic test data, not a password or an
asset-control challenge. Copying the manifest and hash cannot prove current
control of an external web/database resource. A fresh P nonce installed on the
actual asset would be a different, deployment-dependent experiment.

All asset records and delegations use exact signed-payload status identity:
`(org_id, TypeTag, id, version, SHA-256(signed_bytes))`. A missing status answer
is Indeterminate; a revoked or superseded signed object is Reject. The in-memory
status source is synthetic; RQ7 signed-response freshness is not wired into
this new slice.

## RQ3 — independent paths

The caller presents a **complete ordered path** from P's pinned O root to the
Junior. Senior 1 may sign Asset A and Senior 2 may sign Asset B for the **same**
Junior key. Verify each `(path, AssetRecord, AssetRequest, manifest)` separately:

* A request for A needs a complete A path. B's Senior signature cannot be
  combined with A's parent edges or declaration.
* A request for B needs a complete B path. Revoking A's Senior edge does not
  revoke an unrelated B path, although revoking their common CTO edge affects
  both.
* AA17 changes the request's record reference while still presenting A's
  record. AA25 presents the **actual**, validly signed B record, its matching
  Junior request and B manifest alongside the A delegation path. Its expected
  outcome is `Reject / PathMismatch`, even when B's objects have Active status.
* Separate valid paths mean asset-specific alternatives, **not** a two-signature
  threshold policy. A combined action on A and B needs an authorization check
  for each effect; this slice returns no multi-asset Allow.

This first experiment has exactly three role edges per path. Arbitrary-depth
path discovery, conflict aggregation across multiple candidate paths, key
rotation, real asset control proofs, RQ5/RQ6 composition and RQ7 freshness for
these new objects remain future work. It must not be described as full RQ1–RQ3
or production authorization.

## Decision order and experiment

For one presented path: decode all bytes; check pinned root; verify signatures;
check exact parent/issuer/user/record references and role order; check no scope,
class or validity amplification and recompute the manifest hash; check a single
clock snapshot against root, delegations and record; then inspect exact-object
statuses. A **definite, observed** Reject outranks uncertain evidence. If the
status source is unavailable, unseen status answers cannot be inferred.
Neither a failure nor an Indeterminate produces an asset authority.

AA01–AA24 were labelled before their runner and implementation; AA25 was
labelled before its runner. All 25 are now reviewed for those exact fixtures.
The independent test gate compares every literal label to a named mutation.
`cargo run --bin eval_rq123` reports this slice separately from the existing
reviewed 94/15/34/28/34/16/6/17 cases. Synthetic keys derive from named test
seeds; no real asset or network connection is used.
